Answering Any Investor Question, On the Spot

The Ask

Leadership needed to walk into an investor meeting ready for granular questions — not "how's Lagos doing," but "how are VIP customers in Lagos buying Electronics." Any combination of state, category, or segment had to be answerable instantly.

Approach

Built a multi-dimensional pivot table cross-referencing state, category, and customer segment simultaneously, with revenue and transaction counts nested at every level.

Result

Lagos emerged as the clear star performer — ₦30.06M in revenue across 196 transactions, more than double the next-highest state. FCT was a strong secondary market at ₦12.54M, and Kano showed particular strength in Beauty–Skincare and Home–Kitchen.

Is the VIP Program Actually Worth It?

The Ask

Marketing was spending heavily on VIP perks with no clear read on ROI. They needed a numbers-only answer: who actually spends more, VIPs or regular customers — and the segment data lived in a separate customer database from the sales file.

Approach

Used a lookup formula to pull customer segment into the sales data, then built a pivot table comparing revenue, transaction count, and average transaction size across all four segments.

Result

Regular customers drove the most total revenue (₦27.7M), but VIPs had the highest average transaction size (₦185,927) despite the fewest transactions — each VIP interaction carries outsized value. Recommendation: keep investing in VIP for premium and upsell offers, without relying on their low transaction frequency to drive growth.

Finding the Story Behind a Quarter

The Ask

The board wanted a Q3 review comparing September to June — not just totals, but which categories grew, which declined, and by how much, in percentages.

Approach

Compared June and September revenue by category, calculating each category's percentage share of total revenue per period to isolate real movement from overall revenue swings.

Result

Fashion – Men and Home – Kitchen were the clear winners, each gaining share of total revenue; Electronics – Accessories and Fashion – Women declined the most. Paired each finding with a plain-language insight and an actionable recommendation, e.g. bundling accessories with core electronics purchases to offset the decline.

Documenting an Outcome Before Escalation

The Ask

A department head needed to document a possible outlier before referring it to a review committee — full data and context, not assumptions, and handled discreetly.

Approach

Pivoted the outcome data by individual and case complexity to see whether the raw headline number held up once the case mix was accounted for.

Result

The individual's case load was heavily concentrated in the highest-complexity categories, suggesting the outcome variance was driven by case-mix acuity rather than performance — a materially different conclusion than the raw number implied on its own. Delivered as a concise memo recommending further review before any formal action.

A Clear Answer in Under 15 Minutes

The Ask

A regional director suspected Lagos revenue looked "off." The manager needed an answer before a call ended — from a messy, unformatted file, with about 13 minutes on the clock.

Approach

Cleaned and summarized the raw data fast using SUM, COUNTA, AVERAGE, and SUMIF to build a single-glance summary box.

Result

Total revenue was ₦108.39M across 232 transactions, with Lagos alone contributing ₦49.49M — 46% of total revenue, the highest of any location. Lagos wasn't underperforming; it was the strongest region, and the director's concern didn't hold up against the numbers.
